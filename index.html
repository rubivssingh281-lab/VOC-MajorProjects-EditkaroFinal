01 — Craft

Every frame
is a decision.

We obsess over easing curves, timing, and weight until motion stops feeling designed and starts feeling inevitable.

Every project starts with a frame-rate conversation, not a mood board. We map out the exact easing curve a brand's motion should live in — snappy and confident, or slow and deliberate — before a single asset is cut. That decision becomes the DNA of everything that follows: transitions, camera moves, even how long a logo holds on screen.

We treat restraint as a design tool. Most of our work is defined as much by what we chose not to animate as by what moves. It's why our reels tend to feel calmer than they look busy.

02 — Story

Rhythm carries
the narrative.

Pacing is our language. We choreograph attention across seconds so a message lands with the emotional beat it deserves.

We storyboard for emotion first, information second. A campaign might open on stillness and only start moving once it's earned the viewer's attention — every cut, hold, and acceleration is placed deliberately, like beats in a piece of music.

The goal is always the same: by the time the message lands, the audience has already felt something. Pacing is what makes a thirty-second film feel inevitable rather than edited.

03 — Systems

Motion that
scales cleanly.

Reusable motion systems and tokens keep a brand alive and consistent across every screen, platform, and moment.

A single hero film is easy. Keeping that same feeling consistent across a hundred social cuts, product pages, and app transitions is the hard part — so we build motion the way engineers build design systems: as reusable tokens, not one-off tricks.

Easing curves, timing values, and transition rules get documented and handed off, so a brand's motion language survives long after our engagement ends.

HOW WE WORK
From brief to motion.
Discover · Design · Deliver02 / 03
Phase 01 — Listen

We start
with intent.

Before a single keyframe, we map the feeling a brand needs to earn and the moment motion should own.

Every engagement opens with conversation, not a creative brief template. We ask what the brand wants people to feel in the first three seconds, and what moment in their story actually deserves motion — not everything does.

This listening phase shapes the whole project. It's where we decide whether a film should whisper or announce itself, long before any storyboard exists.

Phase 02 — Choreograph

We design
the timing.

Storyboards become motion studies. We prototype easing and rhythm early, so the team feels the beat before polish.

Rather than jumping straight to full production, we build rough motion studies — grey-box animations that test timing and rhythm before a single frame is polished. This lets a client feel the pacing early, when changes are cheap.

Only once the choreography is approved does the real craft work begin: grading, sound design, and final compositing.

Phase 03 — Ship

We deliver
it living.

Final frames arrive with tokens, specs, and Lottie — motion engineers can implement without losing a millisecond.

Delivery isn't a folder of render files. Every project ships with implementation-ready assets — Lottie files, easing specs, and timing documentation — so an internal dev team can build the exact motion we designed, without guessing at values.

We stay available past handoff too, since the first real-world implementation always surfaces a question or two.

THE STUDIO
A small room of specialists.

Philosophy

Restraint over
spectacle.

We believe the best motion is nearly invisible — you feel the smoothness long before you notice the technique.

It's easy to make motion loud. It's much harder to make it feel inevitable — the kind of animation a viewer doesn't consciously register, but would immediately miss if it were removed.

That's the standard every frame is held to here. If a transition draws attention to itself instead of the story, it gets cut, no matter how technically impressive it is.

People

Directors who
animate.

No hand-offs between thinkers and makers. Everyone who shapes the idea also shapes the final frame.

We deliberately stay small and cross-functional. The person who pitches the creative direction is often the same person animating the final cut — so nothing gets lost in translation between concept and execution.

It's a slower way to scale, but it means every project carries a single, consistent point of view from first sketch to final export.

Practice

Fewer, deeper
partnerships.

We take on a handful of projects a year so each one gets the obsession — and the time — to be right.

We turn down far more work than we accept. A small, selective roster means every partnership gets real time — for testing ideas that don't work, revisiting cuts that felt almost right, and pushing past the first acceptable version.

It's a trade-off we're comfortable with: fewer logos on the reel, but a body of work we'd stand behind years later.
